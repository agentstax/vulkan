A Vulkan topic is a named, append-only message log. Producing appends;
consuming never deletes. That single decision — *retain instead of
consume* — is what unlocks everything on this page.

## Fan-out: consumers are independent by construction

The log is shared; each consumer group holds only its own cursor (and
its own delivery rows for failures). Adding a group is invisible to
every other group:

```go
// Three teams, three groups, one topic — zero coordination.
receipts, err := emailConsumer.Register(ctx, "email-receipts",
	registered.Name, topic.SchemaVersion(1), nil)
screening, err := fraudConsumer.Register(ctx, "fraud-screening",
	registered.Name, topic.SchemaVersion(1), nil)
warehouse, err := syncConsumer.Register(ctx, "warehouse-sync",
	registered.Name, topic.SchemaVersion(1), nil)
```

- A slow group falls behind **alone**; nobody else feels it.
- Each group has its own retry budget and its own dead-lettered
  messages ([lifecycle →](/concepts/lifecycle/)).
- A group can declare [bindings](/concepts/routing/) to receive only a
  slice of the topic.

And the part queue-world fan-out can't do: **a new group starts at the
beginning of retained history.** Its cursor starts at zero, so the
consumer you add tomorrow *can* see yesterday — it chews through
history at its own pace and arrives at live traffic. In SQS or
RabbitMQ, the moment a message was consumed it was gone; the fourth
queue you bind next quarter receives only what's published after it
exists.

(The flip side, honestly: there is no "start from now" option today. A
new group on a topic with deep retention will read all of it — register
the group early, or keep retention matched to what you'd want a new
consumer to replay.)

## Replay

For a **new** group, replay is the default behavior above. For an
**existing** group — "re-run Tuesday's window through the fixed
handler" — Vulkan has no rewind verb today:

<ThreadAside label="PROPOSED" title="Rewinding an existing group">
Moving a group's cursor backwards (to an id or a timestamp) is a
proposed capability. The [replay guide](/guides/replay/) is written as
the proposal spec — what the verbs would look like and what they'd
cost. Until it ships, the shipped workaround is registering a fresh
group with the fixed handler and letting it read history.

</ThreadAside>

## Lag: the one health metric

Every group's health is one subtraction — how far its committed cursor
trails the head of the log:

```sql
SELECT g.name AS consumer_group, c.claimed, c.committed,
       (SELECT max(id) FROM message_log_1) - c.committed AS backlog
FROM cursor_1 c JOIN consumer_group g ON g.id = c.consumer_group_id;
```

The same numbers ship as metrics — head, claimed, committed, backlog,
in-flight per group — collected by the metrics worker and readable via
`vulkan metrics list`, because lag is the early warning for every
consumer failure mode: a [stuck handler](/guides/consumer-timeouts/),
an under-provisioned fleet, a
[poison-pill loop](/guides/dead-letters/).

## Retention

Retention is per-topic config, set at registration:

```go
registered, err := messageAdmin.RegisterTopic(ctx, "orders",
	topic.SchemaVersion(1), &topiccontroller.TopicConfig{
		RetentionTTL: 30 * 24 * time.Hour,
	})
```

The default is `0` — keep everything forever. With a TTL set, the topic
janitor drops whole id-range partitions whose newest row has expired —
a `DROP TABLE`, not a million-row `DELETE` — and sweeps the slow tail
that never fills a partition.

<ThreadAside label="TIP" title="The retention cliff, defused by default">
The classic log-world failure is a slow consumer falling off the log:
retention deletes history the group never read. Vulkan's default is the
opposite — **retention never drops past the slowest group's committed
cursor** (`AllowDropPastCommitted: false`). A badly lagging group
blocks cleanup instead of losing data, and the growing backlog is
visible in the lag query above. Set `AllowDropPastCommitted: true` per
topic when staleness beats unbounded disk — Kafka's behavior, opted
into knowingly.

</ThreadAside>
